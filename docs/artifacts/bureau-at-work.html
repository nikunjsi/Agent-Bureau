Bureau
at work

Describe the work. A team of AI staff plans it, does it, and asks you when it matters. You never leave the chat.

Why this exists

AI can already do the work. Almost nobody can supervise it.

One task at a time

Today's AI coding tools work on one thing, in one window, with one person watching. That doesn't scale to a project.

No spending floor

An agent stuck in a loop is invisible until the invoice arrives. Most tools have no cap that actually stops it.

No record

When something goes wrong you can rarely answer "what changed, who changed it, and why." That alone blocks most business use.

What it is

A small company that happens to be software

You hire staff, give them a project, and manage them the way you'd manage people — through a conversation, not a control panel.

01 · Direction

A manager who interviews you

It asks what it needs to know — in batches, not one question at a time — writes a brief in plain English, and waits for your approval before anything begins.

02 · Delivery

Staff who do the work

Several workers run at once on different parts of the project. Their work is checked automatically and combined without collisions.

03 · Judgement

It stops and asks

Ambiguity becomes a question, not a guess. Every question states what each answer will cause, and an unanswered one always resolves the cautious way.

04 · Control

Hard spending limits

Caps per task, per worker, per project and per day. A live meter, a warning before the ceiling, and a worker that parks itself rather than overspending.

05 · Containment

It cannot leave its box

Workers cannot read your credentials, touch system folders, or reach anything outside the project — at any trust level, with no setting that overrides it.

06 · Accountability

A complete record

Every action, decision and cost is logged and attributable, in plain language as well as raw detail. "What changed and why" always has an answer.

How it works

Five steps, and you are only in three of them

In practice

What you'd actually ask it for

Software

Add two-factor sign-in to our customer portal, and make sure existing users aren't locked out.

What comes backA short brief confirming what "not locked out" means, then a plan split into phases. Three workers build, test and review in parallel. You're asked once — about how long the grace period should be — and get working code with a written summary of what changed.

Research

Compare our five biggest competitors' pricing and packaging, and tell me where we're exposed.

What comes backQuestions about which competitors and what "exposed" means to you. Then researchers work in parallel, a reviewer checks the claims, and you get a written comparison with sources — plus an explicit note of anything it could not verify.

Operations

Turn our 200-page policy handbook into a searchable internal FAQ our support team can actually use.

What comes backA proposed structure for your approval before a word is written. Writers draft sections in parallel, a reviewer checks each against the source, and you get a finished document — with every answer traceable to the page it came from.

Why this rather than the alternatives

Four things that are unusual

Nothing leaves your machine

No servers, no accounts

Your project, your data and your conversations stay local. There is no cloud component to review, no vendor holding your code, and nothing to switch off if you stop using it.

Approval is structural

It cannot start early

The brief and the plan each need your yes. This isn't a setting that can be turned off for convenience — it's built into how the system works.

Cost is visible and bounded

You set the ceiling first

A spending limit is part of setup, not an afterthought. The meter is always on screen, and where a tool can't report its cost, it says so rather than showing zero.

Honest about limits

It tells you what it didn't check

Reports state what was verified and what wasn't. Where a safeguard is partial, it's described as partial. You are the final reviewer, and the system is designed to make that easy rather than to replace it.

Where it stands

Built, and being built

Bureau is in active development. This is an honest split rather than a roadmap gloss — the foundations and the safety layer are done and tested; the conversational layer that turns them into a product is next.

Working today

Built and proven
  • Real AI workers run, supervised, on your machine — started, watched and stopped cleanly
  • Workers report progress, ask questions and signal completion back to the system
  • Each worker gets an isolated copy of the project; several run in parallel without collisions
  • Work is inspected, checked and saved centrally — including a secret scanner that can't be disabled
  • Clashing changes stop and raise a question instead of being guessed at
  • The permission system: what each worker may read, write, run or reach, with limits nothing can override

Completing the picture

Designed, in build
  • The manager conversation — interviewing, briefing, planning and reviewing
  • Spending caps and rate-limit handling — in progress now
  • The chat interface you'd actually use day to day
  • The visual office floor showing who's working, stuck, or waiting on you
  • Guided setup, so a new machine reaches a working project without technical steps
  • Non-software teams — research, writing and operations — as installable role sets

What this means for you today: Bureau is not yet something to deploy. It is far enough along that the hard parts — supervision, isolation, containment and cost control — are real and tested rather than promised. The remaining work is the layer you'd touch. We'd rather show you that split than a demo that hides it.

The short version

Delegation, not autocomplete

Most AI tools make one person faster at one task. Bureau is built for the next step — handing over a piece of work the way you'd hand it to a team, with the supervision, spending controls and audit trail that makes handing it over reasonable in the first place.

The conversation is the product. If you can describe the work, you can get the work.
